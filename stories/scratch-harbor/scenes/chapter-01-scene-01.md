# Wren Counts the Losses

## Purpose

Wren reaches the last legible page of the tide book before the flood takes it,
and finds an entry dated after the Ledger Office had already shut for the season.
The scene converts a bureaucratic errand into a crime: someone was writing in
that book when nobody should have been.

## Scene Formula

- **Desire.** Read the final entry before the water reaches the page.
- **Obstacle.** The tide is coming up through the floor, the ink is running, and
  the signature is a smear she cannot resolve.
- **Space geometry.** A tide-house room under the dock boards. Wren at a table
  at the low end of a sloping floor. The door behind her is the threat line;
  the ladder hatch above is the only exit. Water owns the room's low corner.
- **Controlled gaze.** One lantern, one open page. Everything else falls off.
- **Editing rhythm.** Long and slow on the page, then a hard break on the bell.

## Three Details

- **Environmental pressure.** Seawater pushing up between the floor seams, cold
  enough to show her breath; the lantern flame leaning each time it rises.
- **Physical micro-action.** She flattens her thumb on the wet page to stop it
  curling, and holds it there until the knuckle goes white.
- **Sound anchor.** The bell buoy out on the reef, arriving at uneven intervals —
  the scene's clock, and the thing that finally cuts the beat.

## Beats

1. Wren works down the ledger by lantern light, reading entries aloud to keep count.
2. The water reaches the table leg. She lifts the book instead of leaving.
3. The last entry resolves: a date three weeks after the office closed.
4. The signature stays illegible. The bell buoy sounds twice, close together.

## Continuity Notes

- Wren is alive and alone in this scene; Sil Marchetti is referenced, not present.
- The tide book is introduced here and is water-damaged from this point forward.
- Plants the promise `the-marked-ledger` and opens the question
  `who-signed-the-last-entry`.
