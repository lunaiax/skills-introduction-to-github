# Leo Kant

## Appearance

Forty-one. Wiry, weathered, competent hands. A schoolmaster's precision about
small tasks and a schoolmaster's inability to sit still while someone else does
them badly.

Grey at the temples, early. He looks a decade older in the mornings than at
dinner, which Elaya notices and files.

## Wardrobe

Plain and good: a brown tweed jacket with leather at the elbows, an open collar,
walking boots that have been resoled twice. He carries a rucksack, not a case.

## The case

He books two rooms — six and seven, adjoining — for himself and "his brother."

The register says *Kant, L.* and *Lange, S.*, and Elaya sees the two surnames on
one line at seven in the evening on the first day, and says nothing, and that
silence is the episode.

**Room seven's bed is never slept in.** Doro reports it on the third morning,
flatly, and then never mentions it again.

## Function

Episode 5's case, and the one where Elaya's method turns cruel. To keep Frau
Oberst's committee off them, she supplies an alternative explanation for the odd
household at the hotel — a small, plausible, deniable piece of gossip about
another guest entirely.

It works perfectly. It costs Adele Pilz everything she had in the valley.

Leo and Stefan are protected, and leave grateful, and never learn what was paid
for it. Elaya does. This is the high-water mark of her powers and the first time
the audience sees precisely what she is willing to do to a stranger for the sake
of two others.
