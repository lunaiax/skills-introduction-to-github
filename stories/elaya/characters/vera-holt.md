# Vera Holt

## Appearance

Forty-three. Square, direct, no ornament. Cropped grey-brown hair. Reading
glasses she puts on to look at people, which is the opposite of Frau Oberst and
is deliberate.

She is not hostile. She asks her questions the way a person asks for the time.

## Wardrobe

A belted mackintosh over a plain dark jersey and trousers — the first woman in
ten episodes to arrive in trousers, and Frau Oberst notes it. Flat shoes. A
canvas satchel with a notebook in it.

She dresses like someone who came to find something out and go home.

## The case

**She stayed here in 1961, aged eight, with her mother, in room eleven.**

That winter, someone in this building quietly settled a debt her mother could not
pay, put a coat in her size on the end of her bed, and altered a train ticket so
they could stay four days longer. Nobody ever admitted to it. Her mother went to
her grave believing it was the hotel's charity, and Vera went to university on
the difference.

She has come back to find out who did it. She has a list of names, and she works
through them in order, and she is very good at this — she is, in every way that
matters, the first person to use Elaya's own method against Elaya.

## Function

Episode 9's case, and the inversion of the entire series. For the first time
Elaya is the mystery and someone else is doing the reading.

The answer is Aunt Mareau, and Vera gets there on the fourth day, and by then it
does not matter, because what Elaya has understood is the other thing: **that
being on the receiving end of an anonymous kindness is not warm. It is being
handled.** Vera has spent thirty-five years unable to close an account with
someone who refused to be named.

This is what Elaya has done to nine people in eight episodes.

## The detail

Vera is also the reason Elaya goes to the post office out of hours, and finds the
back room open, and finds the ledger.
