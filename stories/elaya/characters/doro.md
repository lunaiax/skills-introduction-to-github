# Doro

## Appearance

Seventies. Short and wide, with forearms like a much younger woman's — forty years
of lifting pans. Grey hair scraped back into a knot with no attempt at style. A
face that decided some time ago that nothing was going to surprise it.

Reading glasses pushed up on her head, always, including when she is reading.

Deaf in the left ear and positions herself accordingly, which reads as her
choosing who to face.

## Wardrobe

Plain slate-grey wool dress, sleeves rolled to the elbow. A white apron that is
somehow always spotless in a working kitchen. Black leather clogs, flat and worn
down at the outer heel. A small silver watch pinned face-up to the apron front,
nurse-fashion.

No jewellery otherwise. No wedding ring, and nobody has ever asked.

## The detail

**The pinned watch.** She does not check clocks; she checks her own chest. She
times everything and has never once used a recipe.

## Description

Seventies. The cook, and the only other person who lives in the hotel. Deaf in the
left ear and uses it ruthlessly — she hears exactly what she chooses to.

Has worked here since Aunt Mareau's time. Cooks every day for a dining room laid
for forty and eaten in by two.

## Function

**Doro knows.** Not the details, but the shape: that Elaya goes out at night, that
guests' things move, that the accounts Elaya reads aloud to her are not the
accounts. She has said nothing for fifteen years.

She is the household's second silence, and the series should let the audience
wonder for several episodes whether her silence is love or leverage. It is love.

## Tic

Salts everything twice, tastes nothing.
