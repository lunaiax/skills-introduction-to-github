# Herr Ferro

## Appearance

Seventy-four. Tall, upright, beautifully shaved, a full head of white hair kept
by a barber who knows him. Everything about him is maintained.

He looks straight at the person speaking. He is looking about ten degrees to
their left, and doing it very well, and has been doing it for two years.

## Wardrobe

Immaculate and unvarying: a charcoal three-piece, a watch chain, polished
oxblood shoes. **The same suit every day**, because he has memorised where it is.
A silk pocket square he folds by touch.

Nothing in his room is ever out of place, and Doro is instructed not to move a
single object, which she assumes is arrogance for four days.

## The case

A letter arrives for him every morning. Same hand, blue ink, no return address.

He takes it to the winter garden and burns it, unopened, in the little iron
stove, and then sits with his coffee for an hour.

Elaya assumes blackmail for three days, which is the wrong answer and the one the
episode wants the audience to reach too. She retrieves a half-burnt one from the
grate at midnight, steams the rest apart, and reads it.

It is from his daughter. It says nothing dangerous. It says what she had for
dinner.

**He is going blind and has not told her**, and he cannot read the letters, and
he will not have them read to him by a stranger, and burning them is easier than
keeping a stack of things that prove it.

## Function

Episode 3's case, and the one that establishes the ethical shape of the series:
Elaya commits a genuine violation — steaming open sealed private mail — and uses
it to do something unambiguously kind. The episode refuses to weigh those against
each other.

Her errand: she invents a "reading hour" for the winter garden, announces it as a
hotel tradition that has never existed, and reads aloud to an empty room that he
happens to be sitting in. He never acknowledges it. He stops burning them.

## The detail

**She keeps one letter.** The half-burnt one. It goes in the drawer in the linen
room, labelled *Ferro, 1963*, and the audience sees her do it.
