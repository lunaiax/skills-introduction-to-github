# Augustin Melle

## Appearance

Fifty-four. Heavy, florid, expansive. Laughs before the end of his own sentences.
Sweats at the hairline in a cold dining room. A gold signet ring he turns with
his thumb when he is talking and stops turning when he is lying, which is the
tell Elaya reads in about four minutes and spends the whole episode confirming.

## Wardrobe

Expensive and slightly loud: a check sports jacket, a cravat, correspondent
shoes. A camel coat with the collar up. He dresses like a man on holiday
performing a man on holiday.

## The case

On the fourth morning he comes down and announces that his wife's diamond brooch
has been taken from their room.

**It is the first accusation ever made against Villa Mareau.**

Elaya knows the brooch was not stolen by any of the three people in the building
— except that she is the third person, and she keeps a drawer of things taken
from guests, and she genuinely cannot remember for two full days whether she took
it. She goes down at two in the morning to check. It is not there. The relief
frightens her more than the doubt did.

The solution: Melle posted the brooch to himself, in the valley, four days before
arriving. He needs it to be stolen. His firm's books do not survive an audit and
the insurance on the brooch does.

The proof is a registered parcel receipt with his signature on it, in the back of
the post office, and Tobin has had it the entire time, and answers only the exact
question he is asked.

## Function

Episode 4's case, the first Elaya and Tobin solve together, and the first time
she has to prove her own innocence to herself. She hands Melle the receipt
privately, in the forecourt, and lets him leave. She never tells his wife.

## The unease

Tobin, closing the drawer, mentions in passing which Thursday she polishes the
bell board on. He has no ordinary way of knowing that.
