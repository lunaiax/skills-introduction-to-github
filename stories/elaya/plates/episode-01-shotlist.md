## The shape of the run

Wide, wide, close, wide, wide, tight, **square**, wide, wide.

Shots 6 and 7 are the two that break pattern, and they are the two that carry the
episode: the linen room is the only asymmetrical frame in the set, and the doorway
is the only one shot square through a threshold. Everything else is the house
grammar.

## Notes on two shots

**Shot 6 breaks the style deliberately.** Per `art-direction.md` §5 the linen room
is the one room that is not symmetrical or beautiful — single hard bulb, tight,
off-centre. It should feel like it belongs in a different film, because it does.

**Shot 7 is the tonal test.** The frame is deliberately pretty: symmetrical, warm,
evenly lit, her expression calm and ordinary, nothing sinister in the lighting.
The wrongness has to come from the *situation*, not the photography — a woman in a
ball gown standing in a sleeping stranger's room at three in the morning, framed
like a nice picture. If it reads as menacing, the lighting has overcooked it. If
it reads as charming, the situation is not being allowed to land.
