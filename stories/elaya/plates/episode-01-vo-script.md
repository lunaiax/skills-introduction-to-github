## How to read the timings

The voice is a **continuous track**; the picture is cut underneath it. Lines are
allowed to run across cuts — that is what binds a montage together, and it is how
Amelie's narration works.

**Only three lines must land exactly.** They are marked ⚑ below. Everything else
can drift a second either way without harm.

---

## The script

**[Overall: warm, dry, unhurried. Someone describing a person they know and are
fond of. Never sombre, never arch. Read as if the facts are mildly interesting,
not as if they are sad — the sadness is the audience's job.]**

---

**0:01** · over the gate

> [breath]
> Her name is Elaya. [tiny pause] She has kept this hotel alone since her aunt
> died, [breath] and told nobody in the valley it's hers.
>
> *[warm, matter-of-fact — an introduction, not a confession]*

---

**0:09** · over the bell board and the hall

> [breath]
> The board went in in 1911, [tiny pause] for a hotel that expected forty people
> [tiny pause] and got them, briefly.
>
> [breath]
> Twenty-one of the bells were cut in 1963. The electrician kept one live, and
> nobody asked him which — [beat] he's been dead eleven years.
>
> *[the "briefly" gets the faintest lift. "Dead eleven years" is dry, almost
> throwaway — the joke is that nobody asked, not that he died]*

---

**0:21** · over the hall and the dining room

> [breath]
> A hundred and six rooms. She keeps the twenty-two that have bells.
>
> [breath]
> Every morning she lays forty places. [beat] Three chairs get drawn in.
>
> *[flat, procedural. Do not lean on "three" — the number does the work alone]*

---

**0:25** ⚑ · the tab drops — **hard anchor, must start on the cut**

> [breath]
> That bell had been rung four times in thirty years.
>
> [beat]
> Twice by mistake. [beat] Once by a child. [longer beat]
>
> And once by a woman who was already dying [tiny pause] and wanted the window
> opened.
>
> *[**Do not perform this.** Four facts in a row, evenly. No warmth added on the
> last one, no slowing down, no drop in pitch. The line is doing all of it. If it
> sounds moved, it is wrong.]*

---

**0:35** · over the night walk

> [breath]
> One tab has had its number rubbed blank. [tiny pause] Her aunt did that, over
> years, [breath] and never said why.
>
> *[curious rather than ominous. A small unexplained thing, noticed and set down]*

---

**0:40** · over the light under the door

> [breath]
> She found him in eleven. [beat] The room he'd asked for, [tiny pause] and hadn't
> been given.
>
> *[quiet satisfaction — the narrator enjoys that she worked it out]*

---

**0:45** ⚑ · the doorway — **hard anchor, must start on the cut**

> [breath]
> At three, she let herself in with the master key [tiny pause] and stood in the
> doorway a while.
>
> [beat]
> She doesn't think about that part afterwards.
>
> *[**Exactly the same temperature as the bell line.** No hush, no weight, no
> slowing. The whole design depends on the narrator finding this unremarkable. If
> the delivery turns ominous the scene becomes a thriller and the series breaks.
> The last clause is said lightly, almost in passing.]*

---

**0:54** · over the corridor

> [breath]
> Then she moved his things two doors along, [breath] and set them out the way he
> would have.
>
> [beat]
> She left no note. [tiny pause] She never does.
>
> *[practical, almost brisk — this is just what she does. "She never does" lands
> with a small shrug]*

---

**0:59** · over breakfast

> [breath]
> He woke in a different room, and never mentioned it. [beat] Neither did she.
>
> *[dry. This is the closest the episode gets to a joke]*

---

**1:04** ⚑ · he leaves — **hard anchor, last line**

> [breath]
> He could have walked around the gate. [beat] Nobody does.
>
> *[gentle, fond, final. Slight warmth permitted here and nowhere else]*

---

**1:08 – 1:18** · the shut door

> *[silence. Ten seconds. Do not fill it.]*

## Breath key

| Mark | Length |
|---|---|
| `[tiny pause]` | ~0.2s — a comma you can hear |
| `[beat]` | ~0.6s — a full stop |
| `[longer beat]` | ~1.0s — used once, before the dying woman |
| `[breath]` | audible intake, kept in the mix |

Keep the breaths in. A narrator who never breathes sounds like a machine, and this
voice has to sound like a person who knows her.

## Word count

211 words over 78 seconds — about 2.7 words per second, slightly slower than v1's
recorded pace. The extra room is in the beats, which is where it should be.
