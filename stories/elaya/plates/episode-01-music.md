# Episode 1 — Music

**Generate elsewhere.** Higgsfield has no music model — its audio tool is
text-to-speech only and explicitly declines music and sound effects. Use Suno,
Udio, ElevenLabs Music or Stable Audio.

## The design decision

The reference is Yann Tiersen — accordion, prepared piano, waltz, ostinato — but
straight Tiersen is too warm for what this episode is doing. The palette needs one
thing he does not have: **something faintly wrong that never resolves.**

**The central rule: the music does not change at the 3am doorway.**

That is the whole trick. When she lets herself into a sleeping stranger's room, the
score keeps playing the same pretty waltz it has been playing since the meadow
gate. It does not darken, slow, drop out, or add a low string. It carries on being
charming while you watch something you should not be watching.

If the music turns ominous there, the scene becomes a thriller and the series
breaks. The score's refusal to comment is what makes the audience uneasy — exactly
like the narrator's.

## Structure across 78 seconds

| Time | Section | What the music does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Theme | Full waltz, curious and light. Establishes the tune |
| 0:25–0:44 | The search | Same tune, stripped to piano ostinato and one held note. Thinner, not darker |
| 0:44–0:58 | **The doorway and corridor** | **Full waltz returns, unchanged.** No adjustment of any kind |
| 0:58–1:08 | Morning | Warmer, accordion leads, resolves almost all the way |
| 1:08–1:18 | The shut door | Thins to a single sustained note, then nothing for the last four seconds |

## Prompt A — main theme, full episode bed

```text
An instrumental waltz in 3/4 at 92 BPM in A minor, in the style of a French
café-cinema score. Lead melody on felt-hammer upright piano, close-mic'd with
audible key noise and hammer felt. Accordion answers the piano phrase. Pizzicato
double bass on the downbeat, light brushed snare, celesta or music box doubling the
melody an octave up. A bowed musical saw enters occasionally on long notes,
slightly under pitch, never quite in tune with the piano.

The melody is simple, circular and a little melancholy — eight bars that return to
where they started without resolving. Charming rather than sad. Curious rather than
grand.

Structure: full arrangement for twenty-five seconds, then strip back to solo piano
ostinato and one held saw note for twenty seconds, then the full arrangement
returns exactly as before with no change in mood or dynamics, then a warmer
accordion-led passage, then thin to a single sustained note and stop.

Recorded small and close, like a quartet in a room, not an orchestra in a hall.
Warm analogue tape character, gentle wow and flutter. No drums beyond brushes, no
synthesisers, no strings section, no swell, no cinematic build, no percussion hits,
no vocals.

Duration: 80 seconds.
```

## Prompt B — sparse variant, for layering under the night section

Optional. Use if the single cue does not thin out enough by itself at 0:25.

```text
A sparse instrumental in 3/4 at 92 BPM in A minor. Solo felt-hammer upright piano
playing a slow circular four-note ostinato, close-mic'd with audible key and pedal
noise. A bowed musical saw holds one long note underneath, slightly under pitch. A
distant music box plays fragments of a melody, incomplete, as if in another room.
Long silences between phrases.

Patient and unresolved. Not tense, not frightening — the music is simply waiting,
the way a large empty building waits.

No drums, no bass, no accordion, no synthesisers, no strings section, no build, no
crescendo, no vocals.

Duration: 25 seconds.
```

## Prompt C — end card

```text
A single sustained note on a bowed musical saw in A, slightly under pitch, fading
slowly to silence over eight seconds. Faint room tone underneath. Nothing else. No
instruments, no melody, no reverb tail beyond a small room, no vocals.

Duration: 10 seconds.
```

## Instrumentation, locked

| Instrument | Role |
|---|---|
| Felt-hammer upright piano | Lead. Close-mic'd, key noise audible |
| Accordion | The answer to every piano phrase. Warmth |
| Celesta / music box | Doubles the melody an octave up. The storybook |
| Pizzicato double bass | Downbeat only. Never bowed |
| Brushed snare | Barely there. No kit |
| **Bowed musical saw** | **The wrong thing.** Long notes, slightly under pitch |

The saw is the whole difference between this and pastiche. It is period-plausible,
genuinely pretty, and never quite in tune — which is the character in one
instrument.

## Never

Synthesisers. String sections. Cinematic swells or builds. Percussion hits on
cuts. Low drones under the unease beats. Choir. Vocals of any kind. Anything that
tells the audience how to feel — the whole design is that nothing does.

## Mix note

The music sits **under** the narration, not around it. When the voice speaks, the
piano drops roughly 6dB and the saw drops out entirely; both return in the gaps.
The two silences — the night walk and the close — are where the score is most
audible, and the only places it should ever be foreground.

## Series use

This theme is Elaya's and recurs in all ten episodes. Two planned variations:

- **Episode 6** — the tune plays in full over Nadja swimming alone, while Elaya
  does nothing. Same melody, and for once it is not about her.
- **Episode 10** — the theme finally resolves. The eight bars land somewhere new,
  once, at the end. It should be the first time in ten episodes that the music
  finishes a sentence.
