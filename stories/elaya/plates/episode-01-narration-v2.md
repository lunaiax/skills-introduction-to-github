# Episode 1 — Narration, v2

Rewrite of the voice-over. **Not yet recorded** — see costs at the bottom.

## What changed and why

**v1 had two failures.**

1. **Elaya was never introduced.** Nine of thirteen clips show her and the
   narration never said who she was or why we were watching her. It described a
   bell board.
2. **The 3am doorway had no words over it.** The single most important image in
   the episode — a woman standing over a sleeping stranger — played under a line
   about how often a bell had rung. The unease beat was left to carry itself.

**The fix is not commentary.** The narrator states what she *does*, flatly, and
the flatness is what makes it land. "At three in the morning she let herself in
with the master key" is an object-level fact with no feeling attached, which keeps
the bible's rule intact and is far worse for being said plainly than any
adjective would be.

**Warmer delivery.** v1 was clipped and statistical. v2 uses contractions, a
dry aside or two, and a rhythm closer to someone telling you about a person they
know. Amelie's narrator is warm and faintly amused; ours was neither.

## Length

**About 177 words, roughly 70 seconds** at speaking pace, against 65 seconds of
picture. Two ways to close the gap:

- **Trim.** Lines 5 and 7 are the most cuttable — 5 is characterful but not
  load-bearing, 7 is covered by the picture. Dropping both brings it to ~155
  words, about 62s.
- **Hold the last shot.** Extend clip 13 by five seconds in the edit. The shut
  door is the one image that improves with more time, and the extra silence at
  the end is free.

Prefer the second. Nothing in the script is padding.

## Note on line 9

This is the line the rewrite exists for. Three facts in order — the hour, the
key, the standing — and then one clause that is the only place in the whole
episode the narrator comes close to her interior life, and even that is phrased
as something she *does not* do.

It should be read at exactly the same temperature as the line about the bell.
