# The Small Errands of Elaya

## Synopsis

Elaya keeps a grand alpine hotel that nobody comes to. Twenty-two rooms, polished
daily, flowers changed, forty places laid in a dining room where three chairs are
ever drawn in. She inherited it from an aunt she has not finished grieving, and
she has told nobody in the valley that it is hers.

She also quietly rearranges the lives of everyone who passes through. She reads
their discarded letters, works out what they will not ask for, and provides it
anonymously, at night, and never once takes credit. The village has a small ghost
story about a woman who paints things in the dark.

Across ten episodes she performs nine of these errands and withholds one — and
the one she withholds works out anyway, which breaks something in her. Meanwhile
the postmaster, a cold and difficult man named Tobin, has been keeping a written
record of everything she has secretly done for eleven years, and has never told
her, and is in love with her.

It is a love story between two people who cannot love anyone without first making
a file on them.

## Tone

**Amelie, but the series knows what Amelie is actually describing.** A woman who
manipulates strangers for their own good, enters rooms she was not invited into,
and takes things that will not be missed. Charming from one angle and frightening
from another, and this series looks from both.

Each episode should be pleasant to sit inside and leave one thing under the skin.
Never horror, never a threat — the unease comes from behaviour, not danger.

## Rules the series keeps

- **The narrator names objects, never feelings.** "The bell was brass. It had been
  rung four times in thirty years." Never "she felt alone."
- **Every errand is anonymous.** Elaya is never thanked on screen until episode 9,
  when it is done *to* her instead.
- **The gloves are looked at every episode and explained in none before 10.**
- **One shot through the meadow gate per episode.** Episode 10 walks around it.
- **One unsettling beat per episode.** Placed late, never resolved that episode,
  never remarked on by any character. See the table below.
- **No swelling music at the end.** The endings are small on purpose.

## The unease, episode by episode

| Ep | The beat that should sit wrong |
|---|---|
| 1 | She moves a sleeping guest's belongings between rooms while he sleeps. |
| 2 | A child sees her painting at 3am in a ball gown and nobody believes him. |
| 3 | She steams open a sealed letter, reads it, reseals it perfectly. |
| 4 | Tobin recites a detail about her he has no ordinary way of knowing. |
| 5 | The reconciliation works, and the lie that achieved it was cruel. |
| 6 | She does nothing, and it happens anyway. The horror is that she is optional. |
| 7 | She arranges a perfect evening for two strangers and watches it from outside. |
| 8 | The drawer in the linen room is opened on camera. Forty labelled objects. |
| 9 | The ledger. Eleven years. Fifty-one entries. Her whole secret life, in his hand. |
| 10 | None. Episode 10 is the one that lets you breathe, and that is its effect. |

## Structure

Ten episodes. Episodic on the surface — one errand each — with a single throughline:
Elaya's own life, which she is the only person in the valley not attending to.

- **1-4 Establish.** The hotel, the method, the village, and Tobin's wrongness.
- **5-6 Test.** Her powers peak, then episode 6 removes the ground: she withholds
  an errand and the outcome arrives without her.
- **7-8 Cost.** What the method protects her from, and what is in the drawer.
- **9-10 Inversion and payoff.** The village performs an errand *for* her, in her
  own method. Then Tobin asks. Then she answers.

## The love story

Tobin is cold, not shy. The ordinary social machinery is simply missing in him.
His love takes the form of eleven years of observation, written down, never used,
never mentioned.

**The audience should be uneasy about him from episodes 3 to 7 and should have to
revise that in 9.** He is not a patient warm man; he is a man with a file. What
redeems it is that she is the same — she reads other people's letters, keeps their
buttons in a drawer, and has never once thought it strange.

They are the only two people in the valley who cannot love anyone without first
making a record of them. That is the romance, and it should read as a diagnosis
before it reads as a happy ending.

## Format

Prose, not screenplay. Ten prose chapters, present tense, third limited on Elaya
with a narrator standing slightly outside her.

The narrator states facts about objects and withholds facts about feelings. The
reader does the feeling; the narrator does the inventory.
