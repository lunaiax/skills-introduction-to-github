# Art Direction — The Small Errands of Elaya

**Version 2.** Rebuilt from the confectionery reference set. Version 1 ran on
faded Austrian realism — overcast, desaturated, peeling, dust sheets. That is
retired. See §0 for what changed and what it costs.

Every image uses the STYLE BLOCK in §3 plus one LOCATION BLOCK from §5, verbatim.
Do not improvise per shot. Drift across ten episodes comes from rewriting the
style each time.

---

## 0. What changed from v1

**Retired:** overcast light, desaturated palette, "faded", "peeling", dust
sheets, "never quaint, never chocolate-box", "grand and municipal", photographic
realism, the cold-and-poverty premise.

**Survives, unchanged:** frontal symmetry, whip pans and fast push-ins, the
wide-close-distorted lens, deep focus, and the meadow gate.

**The palette survives too, which was the surprise.** Elaya's costume — butter
cream gown, powder-blue bow, dusty rose gloves — is drawn from exactly the
colours the reference hotels are built from. She is dressed as the building. Keep
that: in any interior, she should read as *part of the room* rather than a figure
against it, and the one place that fails is the only place it matters.

**The cost, stated plainly.** Episode 1 is written on cold and poverty: twenty-two
rooms with four heated, the unlit stove in room eleven, tables under dust sheets.
None of that survives this pivot and the chapter needs rewriting. See §7.

## 1. The style, named

**Confectionery grand hotel.** Wes Anderson maximalism in an alpine valley.

The building is not faded. It is *immaculate* — polished, lit, chandeliered,
fresh flowers in the hall, every one of its rooms perfect. And almost nobody
comes.

That is the whole emotional engine now, and it is stronger than shabbiness was.
A ruin explains itself. A paradise maintained daily for guests who do not arrive
is a much stranger and sadder thing, and it is precisely Elaya: enormous effort
poured into making things lovely for other people, in exchange for nothing.

### Three rules

**Saturated, never muted.** Rose pink and sage green at full strength, gold as a
structural colour rather than an accent. If a frame reads dusty, it is wrong.

**Warm light indoors, clear light outdoors.** Chandeliers, brass sconces, lamps
with silk shades — interiors glow from their own fittings. Exteriors are bright
and clean under a pale sky. The v1 overcast rule is gone entirely.

**Everything is made, nothing is found.** Painted ceilings, chinoiserie murals,
stained glass, fluted glass, marble checkerboard, ball-fringe on the velvet. The
frame should feel built rather than photographed — the Grand Budapest exterior is
openly a miniature and does not apologise for it.

### The Amélie relationship

Camera and lens are Jeunet's: whip pans, fast push-ins, wide and close with
barrel distortion. Palette and staging are Anderson's. That combination is the
series' actual signature — kinetic hand, doll's-house frame.

What keeps it from being pure pastiche is the *emptiness*. Anderson's hotels
bustle. Ours is beautiful and unpeopled. Wherever a reference shows a crowd,
we show the same room with nobody in it.

## 3. STYLE BLOCK

Append verbatim to every location prompt.

```text
Style: Wes Anderson confectionery maximalism, richly saturated pastels, shot on
Kodak Ektachrome, crisp and clean. Palette of shell pink, rose, sage green,
powder blue, butter cream and polished gold, with black-and-cream marble
checkerboard. Warm interior light from chandeliers, brass sconces and silk-shaded
lamps; exteriors bright under a pale clear sky. Painted and built surfaces —
chinoiserie murals, stained glass, fluted glass, velvet with ball fringe.
Perfectly symmetrical flat frontal composition, dead-centre vanishing point,
wide-angle lens close to the subject with slight barrel distortion. Deep focus.
Doll's-house precision. Format: 16:9.
```

Why each clause: *Ektachrome* replaces Portra — it gives saturated, clean,
slightly artificial colour where Portra gave muted pastels, and that swap alone
does much of the work. *Dead-centre vanishing point* is the Anderson signature and
the single most recognisable thing to hold. *Doll's-house precision* pushes toward
the built, miniature quality. Wide-close-distorted and deep focus carry over from
v1 unchanged.

## 4. Staging rules

- **Perfect symmetry, dead centre.** One-point perspective, vanishing point in the
  middle of frame. Doors, corridors, facades, bars, staircases — all square on.
- **Whip pan between centred compositions**, fast push-in on a held wide. The
  motion vocabulary in §5A is unchanged from v1.
- **Wide and close**, mild barrel distortion at the edges.
- **The hotel is immaculate and empty.** Every surface cared for; no people. This
  is the inversion of the reference set and it is the whole point.
- **Elaya matches the room.** Her cream, blue and rose read as part of the decor.
  She is camouflaged in her own house — which is another way of saying nobody
  sees her.
- **Checkerboard floors recur.** Hall, terrace, bathrooms, vestibule. A motif.
- **Gold script signage.** Room names in hand-lettered gold script, Anderson-style.
- **One shot through the meadow gate per episode.** Unchanged. Episode 10 walks
  around it.

## 5. Fixed locations

Use verbatim, then append the STYLE BLOCK.

### 5.1 Villa Mareau — exterior

```text
A grand Jugendstil spa palace hotel in an alpine valley, seven storeys,
photographed straight on. The entire building is shell pink — pink render, pink
dressed stone, pink balustrades, pink terrace — with no white and no contrasting
trim anywhere. Art Nouveau detailing: curved bays, sinuous stone mouldings, tall
arched windows with iron tracery, iron-caged balconies with organic scrollwork, a
large circular oculus above the central entrance, and two domed corner turrets in
dark slate with flagpoles. A long glazed winter garden runs down one flank.

In front, a vast pink paved forecourt with a stone fountain at its centre, reached
by twin curved stairs over an arched undercroft, and beyond it terraced formal
gardens with clipped topiary and rose beds running down toward a meadow. Grey
mountains and pine slopes behind under a pale clear sky.

The upper floors are shuttered and dark. Only the lower windows are lit. The
building is immaculate, freshly rendered, and far too large for anyone to be
living in it.
```

Canon: **pink on pink on pink — no white trim.** That single note is what separates
a confection from a civic building, and it was the error in v2. Seven storeys, 106
rooms, and the shuttered upper floors must be visible in every wide shot.

**Photographic, never illustrated.** The reference for the massing is painted
background art; the reference for the *rendering* is not. Real light, real
material, real depth of field.

### 5.2 The Meadow Gate

```text
A pair of tall gilded Art Nouveau iron gates, sinuous and scrolled with organic
tracery, standing open at the foot of the hotel's terraced gardens where the
formal planting gives way to open alpine meadow. There is no wall or fence on
either side — the wall came down decades ago and the gates stand alone in the
grass. A worn footpath runs through the opening; the grass to either side is
undisturbed. The pink palace hotel rises behind on its terraces, mountains beyond,
pale clear sky.
```

Canon: the iron is now **gilded**, not rusted. The undisturbed grass either side
is unchanged and is still the whole story.

### 5.3 Entrance hall

```text
The grand hall of an alpine spa hotel: a sweeping double staircase in shell pink
and gold with a brass balustrade, a black-and-cream marble checkerboard floor, a
vast crystal chandelier, a painted ceiling of pale blue sky with swallows, sage
green chinoiserie wall panels with pink blossom, and a polished mahogany
reception desk with a brass counter-bell and a wall of pigeonholes behind it.
Fresh flowers in a tall urn. Perfectly symmetrical, entirely empty.
```

### 5.4 The kitchen and the bell board

```text
A grand hotel kitchen in cream and sage green with brass rails, copper pans, a
long marble-topped table, and checkerboard floor tiles. High on the wall, a
servants' bell board from 1911: twenty-two small brass tabs in two rows in a
glazed gilt-and-mahogany case, each with a room number engraved in copperplate
beneath it. One tab in the second row, seventh from the left, has had its number
rubbed smooth and blank. Warm light from a brass pendant lamp.
```

Canon unchanged: 22 tabs, two rows, blank one **second row, seventh from left**.
The case is now gilt rather than plain dark wood.

### 5.5 The dining room

```text
A hotel dining room built for forty in rose pink and gold, with tall arched
windows down one side, a moulded and painted ceiling, three crystal chandeliers,
and round tables under white linen with gold-rimmed china and heavy silver. Every
table is fully laid and pristine. One table near the window has three chairs
drawn in; the rest are perfectly squared and untouched. Empty.
```

Canon changed: the dust sheets are gone. **Every table is laid, all forty places**,
and only three chairs are ever drawn in. Laying a room for forty every day for
three people is now the gesture that says what the dust sheets used to.

### 5.6 Room Eleven

```text
A hotel bedroom in sage green and cream striped silk wallpaper, with a
four-poster bed in white linen with gold trim, a row of small gilt-framed
pictures above the headboard, a marble washstand with gold fittings, a
silk-shaded lamp on each side, and a bell rope of rose silk beside the bed. One
tall window with the shutter half closed. Immaculate and unoccupied.
```

Canon changed: no longer cold and bare. It is **as lovely as every other room** —
and Havel still asks for it by number, which now means something different and
better. See §7.

### 5.7 Aunt Mareau's door

```text
A closed double door at the end of a long upper corridor, in shell pink with
gilded mouldings, an oval panel on each leaf, and gold handles. No room number.
The corridor runs dead away from camera with a checkerboard runner, brass sconces
down one wall, and a painted sky ceiling above.
```

Canon: **closed door only, until episode 8.** The interior is not designed before
then, and when it opens it must be disappointingly ordinary — the one plain room
in a magnificent hotel.

### 5.8 The Candy Bar

```text
A small hotel bar in rose pink and powder blue: a curved bar counter faced in
glazed blue tile with a pink marble top, gold-framed back-bar shelves, pink
velvet stools on brass legs, a chinoiserie mural of palms and pavilions in blue
on cream, and a row of pendant lamps with pink ostrich-feather shades. A sage
green door with "Candy Bar" in gold script, half hidden by a raspberry velvet
curtain with ball fringe. Warm lamplight. Empty.
```

New location, from the reference set. Canon: the green door with gold script
signage and the raspberry velvet curtain are its identifying pair.

### 5.9 The Elevator

```text
A hotel elevator in shell pink with gilded mouldings, oval panels, fluted glass
inserts in the doors, and gold handles. Above the doors, a brass floor indicator
with round enamel buttons reading G, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. A brass sconce with
a pleated silk shade on the wall beside it. Warm light, checkerboard floor.
```

New location. Canon: floors **G through 8**, in that order.

### 5.10 The Pool Terrace

```text
A hotel terrace with a rectangular swimming pool, a red-and-cream checkerboard
tiled deck, rows of folded cream parasols and coral loungers, clipped topiary in
gold urns, and a low balustrade. The pink and cream hotel rises behind, with grey
mountains beyond under a pale clear sky. Perfectly symmetrical. The loungers are
all empty and the water is completely still.
```

New location. Canon: still water, folded parasols, no swimmers.

### 5.11 The Post Office

```text
A small alpine village post office in sage green and cream: a polished wooden
counter with a brass grille, a wall of numbered pigeonholes, a brass scale,
string, a franking stamp, and racks of sorted letters. A man's grey wool coat on
a peg by the door. Warm light from a single brass pendant. Snug and correctly
sized for the people who use it.
```

Canon: the pigeonholes are sorted **Friday to Monday, left to right**. This is the
only interior in the series that is the right size for its occupants, and it
should feel like relief after the hotel.

### 5.12 The Bus Shelter

```text
A small timber bus shelter at the edge of an alpine village, with a bench, a
painted timetable board, and a single-pitch roof. Freshly painted in a pink that
is very slightly too coral, standing beside an older wall in the correct shell
pink, so the mismatch between the two pinks is just visible. Clean road, verge of
wildflowers, mountains behind under a pale clear sky.
```

Canon changed: the mismatch is now **pink against pink**, not yellow against
yellow, and must be *almost* right. The joke dies if it is obviously different.

### 5.13 The Forecourt

```text
A vast pink paved forecourt in front of a Jugendstil spa palace hotel, with a
carved stone fountain at its centre, a low pink balustrade around the edge, and
twin curved stairs descending over an arched undercroft to the gardens below.
Clipped topiary in stone urns. The pink hotel facade rises behind, mountains
```

Canon: built to receive forty arrivals at once and receives none. The emptiness of
this space is the single most efficient statement the series has, and it should
open at least three episodes.

### 5.14 The Winter Garden

```text
A long glazed Art Nouveau conservatory running down the flank of a pink palace
hotel: curved iron ribs and glass, tall palms and ferns in glazed pots, cane
furniture with rose cushions, a chequered tile floor, and pink blinds half drawn
against the light. Warm and humid and green. Empty.
```

Canon: the only warm and green room in the series. Elaya waters it daily. Used in
episodes 6 and 9.

## 5A. Motion vocabulary

Unchanged from v1. For the video pass — a still cannot contain a camera move; it
carries the *lens* and the *framing the move lands on*. Never prompt "whip pan"
into a still image.

- **Whip pan between centred compositions.** Object to face, face to object.
  Always lands square on.
- **Fast push-in on a held wide.** The empty room, then hard in to Elaya. At
  least once an episode.
- **Snap-back.** Push in, cut back out to the same wide, nothing changed. Reserve
  for episodes 6 and 7.
- **Insert whip.** The narrator names an object; the camera snaps to it and holds
  one beat too long.

No handheld, no drift, no slow motion. Every move fast, motivated, locked off at
both ends. Episode 10 gives up the whip pan and plays in static wides.

- Saturated, warm indoors, bright and clear outdoors. **No overcast, no dust, no
  fade.**
- Palette inside the locked eight. Dusty rose `#E3AFAF` reserved for the gloves.
- Perfectly symmetrical, dead-centre vanishing point.
- Wide lens, close in, mild barrel distortion.
- **Immaculate and empty.** Every surface cared for; nobody there.
- Checkerboard where the bible calls for it.
- Bell board: 22 tabs, two rows, blank one second row seventh from left.
- Gate: gilded, and the grass either side undisturbed.
- No motion blur in a still.

## 7. What this costs the prose

Episode 1 was built on cold and poverty and does not survive intact. Specifically:

- "twenty-two rooms and four of them heated" — gone
- the unlit stove in room eleven, and Elaya lighting it at 3am — gone
- "a dozen round tables under dust sheets" — replaced by forty places laid daily
- "she has never had the money to change it" — gone

**The replacement errand, which is better.** Havel asks for room eleven by number.
Elaya gives him four. He goes to eleven anyway at night — not because it is cold
and he is stubborn, but because it is the room he stayed in, and it is *exactly as
it was*, because she keeps all twenty-two that way. He pulls the bell rope without
meaning to. What she does at 3am is not light a stove; it is move him into eleven,
so that he wakes where he meant to be.

The gesture is identical — anonymous, unacknowledged, precisely right. Only the
poverty is gone, and the loneliness is untouched.

**Still true after the pivot:** the house is too big for her, the one live bell
answers eleven, her aunt rewired it and scratched the number off, the door at the
end of the corridor stays shut, and she takes off nothing.
