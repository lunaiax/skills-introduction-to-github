# The Linen Room

## Description

Second floor, no window, one bulb. Deep shelves of hotel linen in cream and gold,
stacked to the ceiling and perfectly squared. It smells of starch and cedar.

This is the only room in a hundred and six that is hers.

## The Drawer

Bottom left, under the sheets, there is a shallow drawer.

It holds objects taken from guests over fifteen years. A pearl button. A hairpin.
A bus ticket to a town two valleys over. A pressed flower with a date. A child's
milk tooth in a twist of paper. Forty-odd items, each labelled in the Register's
hand with a name and a year.

Nothing in it is valuable. Nothing in it was missed.

## Why It Matters

The single most damaging object in the series, and it is not damaging because it
is a crime. It is damaging because of what it means: she is collecting proof that
people were here, because she cannot make anyone stay.

If the drawer were found, the village's reading of her would change permanently
and it would not be wrong.

Seen in episodes 1, 4 and 8. Opened on camera in 8. Never explained aloud.

## Rules For The Camera

The one room in the hotel that is **not** symmetrical and not beautiful. Shot
tight, off-centre, single hard bulb. It should look like it belongs in a different
series — because it does.
