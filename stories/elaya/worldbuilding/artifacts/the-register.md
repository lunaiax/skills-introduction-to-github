# The Register

## Description

The hotel's guest book, begun 1911. Every person who has ever slept in Villa
Mareau, in one continuous copperplate hand across sixty years and four thousand
names.

It is one hand because Aunt Mareau wrote it in the hand of the woman before her,
and Elaya writes it in her aunt's. She taught herself the forgery at eleven and is
better at it than her aunt ever was.

## Why It Matters

An unbroken hand across three women is the series' quietest and strangest idea: a
building that keeps its own records through whoever happens to be standing in it.

Elaya has read the whole thing. She knows the dead by name and has favourites
among them. She talks about 1937 guests in the present tense and does not notice
doing it.

## Rules For The Camera

Insert whip, always. The narrator names a year; the camera snaps to the page.
Never a lingering hero shot — it is furniture to her, and that is the point.
