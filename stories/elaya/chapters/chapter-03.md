# Episode 3: The Letters He Burned

## Logline

An immaculate old man receives a letter every morning in the same hand and burns
it unopened. Elaya assumes blackmail for three days and is wrong.

## The Guest

**Herr Ferro**, 74. Beautifully maintained, the same suit every day, everything
in his room untouchable.

## The Case

1. One letter daily, blue ink, no return address, same hand.
2. Burned in the winter garden stove, unopened, then an hour of sitting.
3. He instructs Doro to move nothing in his room. She reads it as arrogance.
4. He looks straight at whoever is speaking — and about ten degrees to their left.
5. He counts the stairs. Silently. Elaya catches him at it on the fourth day and
   recognises it instantly, because she counts everything too.

**Wrong answer (days 1–3):** blackmail. **Right answer:** he is going blind, he
has not told his daughter, he cannot read her letters, and a stack of unread mail
is a stack of proof.

## The Method

Elaya retrieves a half-burnt letter from the grate at midnight and steams the
remainder of a fresh one apart the next morning. It contains nothing dangerous
whatsoever. It says what his daughter had for dinner and that the dog is fat.

**This is the episode where the audience must feel the violation clearly**, and
then watch it produce something they cannot object to.

## The Errand

She invents a hotel tradition. A card appears at the desk announcing that the
winter garden has a reading hour at five, as it has since 1911, which is a lie,
and she reads aloud from the newspaper and from whatever else is to hand — into
an empty room that Herr Ferro happens to be sitting in.

He never acknowledges it. He stops burning them.

By the last day there are nine letters open on his writing table, weighted with a
saucer, and he asks her to read the newspaper more slowly.

## Tobin

The reseal is perfect. Tobin, who has handled every one of these envelopes, sees
that one has been opened and closed again and says absolutely nothing — to her,
to anyone.

**He writes it down.** Entry 47. The audience sees the ledger for the first time
here, from behind, without knowing what it is.

## Unease

She keeps one. The half-burnt letter goes in the bottom left drawer of the linen
room, labelled *Ferro, 1963*, and the shot holds on her hand doing it.

## Series Thread

Tobin established as the other half of the method. The drawer named but not
opened.
