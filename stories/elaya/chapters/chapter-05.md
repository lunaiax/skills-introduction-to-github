# Episode 5: The Room Nobody Slept In

## Logline

Two men book adjoining rooms as brothers. One bed is never slept in. When the
village committee starts asking questions, Elaya protects them by giving it
somebody else to look at.

## The Guests

**Leo Kant**, 41, wiry and precise. **Stefan Lange**, 38, ill, very funny, and
the only guest in ten episodes who makes Doro laugh.

And **Adele Pilz**, 60, widow, floral cotton and a lilac cardigan, on the same
eleven-day holiday she has taken every year for eleven years.

## The Case

There is no crime. The case is that Elaya solves the guests in one evening and
then spends nine days deciding what to do about it.

1. Two surnames on one line of the register. *Kant, L.* and *Lange, S.*
2. Room seven's bed unruffled on the third morning. Doro reports it flatly, once,
   and never again.
3. Lange asks for the room with the best view and takes the one with the shortest
   staircase.
4. Kant looks ten years older at breakfast than at dinner.
5. Neither of them has ever said the word *brother* out loud. It is only written.

Elaya moves Lange to the good room on the floor below without being asked and
without mentioning it. He understands exactly what has happened and says nothing.
It is the closest thing to a thank-you the series permits before episode 9.

## The Turn

**Frau Oberst's committee begins asking about the hotel's guests.** Not cruelly.
Procedurally, which is worse, and which is how the valley has always done it.

Elaya could say nothing and hope. Instead she does what she is good at: she
supplies the committee with a better story. Small, plausible, deniable, and
entirely invented — about the widow in room nine, who talks to everyone, and who
has no one to correct the record.

It is beautifully done. It takes four days. It works completely.

## The Cost

Adele Pilz is asked, kindly, to shorten her stay. She does not understand why.
She is too polite to ask twice.

The last image of the episode is Adele at the bus shelter with her case, eleven
days early, waving at nobody — and Elaya at the top of the drive, watching, not
going down.

Kant and Lange leave grateful, and never learn what was paid for them.

**She does not come back the following year.** The series never mentions it
again. Her name is in the Register eleven times, in Elaya's hand.

## Unease

The reconciliation of the episode is real. The method was cruel. Nobody says so,
and the person who paid never finds out there was a bill.

## Series Thread

The high-water mark of her powers, and the first time the audience sees what she
will spend on a stranger. Tobin sees the whole of it and writes it down without
comment. Entry 50.
