# Episode 9: Somebody Comes Looking

## Logline

A guest arrives with a notebook and a list of names, determined to find out who
quietly saved her family in 1961. For the first time Elaya is the case, and
somebody else is doing the reading.

## The Guest

**Vera Holt**, 43. Mackintosh, flat shoes, canvas satchel. The first woman in ten
episodes to arrive in trousers. She asks her questions the way a person asks for
the time.

## The Case

In the winter of 1961 Vera was eight, in room eleven, with a mother who could not
pay. Somebody settled the bill, left a coat in her size on the end of the bed, and
altered a train ticket so they could stay four days longer. Her mother died
believing it was the hotel's charity. Vera went to university on the difference.

She has been looking for thirty-five years, on and off, and she is very good at
it. She works exactly the way Elaya works — from objects, from registers, from
what people did not think to hide.

**She is Elaya, aimed at Elaya.**

1. She asks for room eleven by number. The second guest in the series to do so.
2. She has the coat. It is in the satchel. It has a maker's label from a shop in
   the valley that closed in 1964.
3. She works through the Register and stops at the join — the place where one
   continuous copperplate hand is very slightly too continuous.
4. She asks Elaya, pleasantly, on the third evening, how old she is. Then does the
   arithmetic out loud. Then apologises.

**Answer: it was Aunt Mareau.** Vera gets there on the fourth day. Elaya helps
her, and hands her the entry, and watches her read it.

## The Real Discovery

It does not help. That is the point of the episode.

Vera has spent thirty-five years unable to close an account with someone who
refused to be named, and now the someone is dead and it is still open, and she
sits in the hall with the answer in her hands and it is worth nothing.

**Being on the receiving end of an anonymous kindness is not warm. It is being
handled.**

Elaya has done this to nine people in eight episodes.

## The Inversion

Meanwhile the village, without telling her, repaints the east shutter of the
villa. At night. In the correct colour. No note.

**Peter Lind organises it** — twelve now, and the only person who ever knew — and
he gets the others to believe him by producing a page, dated, in the postmaster's
hand, recording exactly what a boy said he saw at three in the morning four years
ago.

## The Ledger

Elaya goes to the post office out of hours. The back room is open.

Six years. Fifty-one entries. Dates, weather, and what the person it landed on
did afterwards. Never used. Never mentioned.

Tobin comes in and finds her holding it, and does not explain, and does not
apologise, and asks her the question he has been carrying since episode 7 —
which is not about the ledger.

**He asks about the gloves.** Directly, kindly, once.

She leaves the room.

## Unease

The ledger itself, page by page, in his hand, in silence.

## Series Thread

Everything is now on the table except the answer.
