# Episode 4: The Theft That Did Not Happen

## Logline

A guest reports a diamond brooch stolen from his room — the first accusation ever
made against Villa Mareau. Elaya cannot immediately prove to herself that she did
not take it.

## The Guests — a honeymoon

**Augustin Melle**, 54, loud, expansive, turns his signet ring while talking and
stops turning it when he lies. **Iris Melle**, 31, married to him **six weeks**,
dressed entirely in clothes he chose, who has never been told what the brooch is
worth.

This is their wedding trip. He introduces her as "the new Frau Melle" four times
in two days, including to Doro, who was standing there the first three.

## The Case

1. The room was locked and there are four keys: two guest, one Doro, one master.
2. The brooch was insured six weeks ago, which he mentions on the first morning,
   unprompted, before it is stolen.
3. He describes it in too much detail, twice, identically. Rehearsed.
4. Iris says, mildly, that she has never worn it — a brooch she supposedly owns,
   six weeks into a marriage. Nobody in the room registers it except Elaya.
5. Her wedding ring is loose. She turns it back onto her knuckle about forty
   times an hour and has stopped noticing she is doing it.
6. His signet ring stops turning when he says the words *last night*.
7. Tobin has a registered-parcel receipt, signed, dated four days before the
   Melles arrived, addressed to Melle at the poste restante in the village.

**Solution:** he posted it to himself before arriving. His firm's books do not
survive an audit; the insurance does.

## The Two Detectives

The first case Elaya and Tobin work together, and the template for the rest of
the series: **she has the building, he has the valley.** He is standing at her
desk franking the Melles' outgoing post on a Tuesday morning when she asks her
first real question, and the whole thing takes place across a mailbag. She knows what a guest
carried in. He knows what came for them, what they posted, who signed.

He volunteers nothing. He answers exactly the question asked. She has to know
what to ask. Neither of them has enjoyed anything this much in years and neither
will say so.

## The Private Case

Two nights in, at two in the morning, Elaya goes down to the linen room and
opens the bottom left drawer, and checks — because she keeps forty labelled
objects taken from guests over fifteen years, and for two full days she genuinely
does not know whether the brooch is among them.

It is not. **The relief frightens her more than the doubt did.**

## Resolution — and the interference

She hands Melle the receipt in the forecourt, privately, at seven in the morning,
and lets him leave without a word to anyone.

**Not to protect him. To protect the marriage he is using as a cover**, because
when the fraud lands it lands on Iris: six weeks married, no money of her own, and
a name she has had for six weeks that would be in a newspaper by Christmas.

Then Elaya interferes, twice, and neither is asked for. The fur stole comes back
brushed and re-lined. And the wedding ring goes down to the village jeweller
overnight, is resized, is paid for in cash out of Elaya's own money, and is back
on the washstand before Iris wakes.

Iris finds it fits. She assumes her husband did it. She is warmer to him for two
days, and they leave holding hands.

**Elaya has just improved a marriage she knows to be a trap.** The series does not
comment, and neither does the narrator, and that is the episode's last cut.

## Unease

Closing the drawer behind the post office counter, Tobin remarks — not as a
gambit, simply as a fact in a sentence — that Thursday is a poor day to call
because she will be polishing the board.

He has no ordinary way of knowing that.

## Series Thread

Ledger entry 49. The romance is now visible to the audience and to neither of
them.
