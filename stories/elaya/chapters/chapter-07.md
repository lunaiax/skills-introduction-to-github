# Episode 7: A Table for Two Other People

## Logline

Two strangers check in the same week. Elaya works out that one of them gave the
other away in 1934, and builds the most elaborate errand of her life rather than
say one sentence to Tobin.

## The Guests

**Julian Aubry**, 29, who comes for one night every year on the sixteenth of
October and does not know why. **Clara Mon**, 58, first visit, four nights, and a
return ticket booked for two.

## The Case

1. They stand the same distance from people. Too far. Elaya sees it across the
   hall on the first evening, before she has a single other fact, and cannot
   unsee it.
2. Julian's five entries are in Elaya's hand. A sixth, in 1934, is in the hand
   *before* her aunt's — a different surname, a party of two, aged five months.
3. Clara's 1934 entry: party of two on arrival, **party of one on departure.**
   The Register records the number. The Register has always recorded the number.
4. The return ticket for two. She bought it without deciding to.
5. Neither of them has ever asked the hotel a question about the other.

**She solves it on the second evening.** Six episodes ago that would have been
the end of the story. It is not, because now she has to decide whether her
knowing anything makes any difference at all, and she has stopped believing it
does.

## The Errand

She does it anyway, and enormously, because doing it is the only thing she knows
how to be.

A dinner. A seating plan built so that neither can leave without passing the
other. A bottle in the right year, found in the cellar and decanted. A gramophone
record that was played in this hall in 1934, which she finds because the Register
records the weather and Doro remembers the music. A power cut at nine, arranged.

It works. They talk for four hours. Neither ever learns that anything was
arranged, and neither ever learns the fact about each other — **she engineers the
conversation, not the revelation.** They part as two people who had a good
evening.

## The Cowardice

Tobin has come up the hill for one reason. He has a sentence ready. He has had it
ready for some weeks and everyone can see it but her.

She spends eleven hours on other people's evening and watches the last four of it
from the garden, through glass, in the rain.

He waits in the forecourt until eleven and then walks home.

## Unease

She is standing outside her own life, in the wet, arranging the inside of it for
strangers, and she arranged that too.

## Series Thread

The romance stalls, and it stalls by her hand, deliberately, using every skill
the first six episodes taught the audience to admire.
