# Episode 8: Forty Things That Belong to Other People

## Logline

A tax assessor gives nine days' notice that he will list and value every object in
every room. Elaya has nine days to give back a drawer full of things she stole.

## The Guest

**Anselm Ruck**, 54. Grey suit with no opinion in it. Rimless spectacles. Holds
his hat indoors. Entirely courteous, thanks her for each room, never raises his
voice — and requires access to all one hundred and six.

## The Case, Run Backwards

**The guilty party is the detective.**

The bottom left drawer in the linen room holds forty objects taken from guests
over fifteen years. Each is labelled in Elaya's hand with a surname and a year.
That is all the information there is.

The episode is nine days of solving her own crimes from her own filing:

- A tortoiseshell comb. *Barre, 1954.* Barre is dead. His widow is not.
- A single cufflink. *Weiss, 1961.* Elaya has to work out from the Register which
  of two Weisses, on which visit, and whether he was the one who came back.
- A child's rubber elephant. *Ostrow, 1958.* The child is nineteen now.
- A tram ticket. *Ferro, 1963.* Five episodes ago. Still warm.

She returns twenty-nine of them by post, unsigned, over nine nights. **Tobin
handles every single parcel and asks nothing**, and the audience watches him
recognise the handwriting on all twenty-nine and stamp them anyway.

Six she cannot place. Four she cannot bring herself to send.

## The Brooch

One of the forty is a small gold brooch labelled **Oberst, 1959.**

Frau Oberst — who has campaigned against this hotel for a decade, who has called
Elaya's gloves theatrical in public — stayed here once, for two nights, and Elaya
took something from her room.

It goes back anonymously on the seventh night. Frau Oberst finds it on her own
doorstep and, in the only moment of the series where she is not composed, sits
down on the step.

She never mentions it. She continues to campaign. This is not a redemption; it is
a fact.

## The Door

Ruck needs every room. On the eighth day they reach the end of the second-floor
corridor: shell pink, gilded, no number, the one bell in the building that does
not work.

She opens it.

**Aunt Mareau's room is ordinary.** A bed, a chair, a hairbrush, a window with
the shutter a third open. Nothing is hidden. Nothing is explained. Ruck lists
eleven objects and values them at very little, and Elaya stands in the doorway
and finds she has nothing to feel, and that is the worst thing that happens in
the series.

## The Fortieth Object

In the drawer, underneath the rest, wrapped in tissue: **one pink opera glove.
Left hand. Elbow length.**

The label is not in Elaya's handwriting. It is in her aunt's, and the name on it
is not the name of any guest.

She does not send it. She puts it back. She closes the drawer.

## Unease

The drawer, open on camera, counted, for a long time.
